The Arrival Round

We roll into the ranch Thursday and go straight at the Gauntlet. There’s a placeholder tee time at 1 PM, but the course is wide open — we tee off whenever the crew is in. Warm-up round: it doesn’t count toward the five tournament rounds, but bragging rights are live.

The Course
Fantasy Golf on the Ridges
Unlike any other course in Oregon.
THE STORY
OPENED 2017
Why It Exists
  • Silvies owner Dr. Scott Campbell wanted a true fantasy course — holes you’d see in video games or on fantasy posters, like Bud Chapman’s Infamous 18 Golf Holes.
  • It runs along a series of ridges too steep for carts — but not for goats.
  • Designed by Dan Hixson. 7-hole public executive course, plus a bonus hole.
THE BONUS HOLE
PAR 2
The Only Par 2 in Oregon
  • A 25-yard-ish hole with an extra-large cup on top of a ridge — putt it or chip it.
  • Par it, and you may move your ball up to 7.7 inches in any direction on every remaining hole.
  • There’s beer waiting at the bonus hole. Obviously.
KNOW BEFORE
YOU TEE OFF
Bring Balls. Plural.
  • Hole 2 is all carry — if you can’t consistently fly it 160 yards, reload and laugh.
  • Don’t play it serious. You will lose golf balls — fill your pockets.
  • Hilly but short. Strip your bag down to the clubs you actually need.
  • Soft spikes only. No yardage markers except at the tee.
GOAT CADDIES
MORNINGS ONLY
The Famous Ones
  • This is the goat-caddie course — special harnesses carry two bags with up to six clubs, balls, drinks, and peanuts for the goats.
  • Caddies are limited and work mornings only, assigned by lottery the night before — so our Thursday-night round is a carry-your-own affair. Our goat caddies come Sunday on the Egan Course.
  • Caddie goats (Boer × Kalahari Red crosses raised on the ranch) serve out their caddying years and retire to adoption — and a portion of green fees supports local conservation.
